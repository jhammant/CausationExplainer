The Causation Meter.

If you find one complicated thing, it might be luck. If you find many identical complicated things, something has been making them. Assembly Theory turns that intuition into a proposed physical scale: assembly index × copy number.

§ the claim

Causation leaves a material fingerprint.

The paper argues that causation is not just a story we tell after the fact. It can be measured in matter. A thing with a high assembly index embodies a long chain of contingent construction steps. A large copy number says that chain is not a one-off accident: some persistent mechanism is producing it.

The interesting zone is not merely complexity. Random strings can be complex. Nor merely abundance. Simple salts can be everywhere. It is the combination: copied objects that are hard to make.

A ≈ Σ nᵢ eaᵢ / N

Toy version used here: assembly field rises when objects have both high assembly index aᵢ and high copy number nᵢ. The real theory is more careful; this app is a visual intuition pump, not a lab instrument.

§ interpretation

The four regimes.

Common + shallow

Snow, salt, water, CO₂. Lots of copies, but few assembly steps. Physics can flood the universe with them.

Deep + rare

A weird random polymer may be hard to build, but if it appears once it is not yet evidence for a lineage.

Deep + copied

The paper's hot zone. DNA motifs, proteins, tools, chips: complicated objects repeatedly made by selection, agency, or technology.

Low + rare

Mostly noise. Neither hard to make nor common enough to demand an explanation.

§ toy universe

Random drift struggles to climb. Selection ratchets.

Run a tiny string-world. In random mode, strings mutate without preference. In selection mode, strings containing a motif are copied more often. The point is not biology realism; it is the ratchet: once a useful pattern exists, the future is biased by the past.

§ assembly calculator

Try the old trick: count the reusable steps.

Type a repeated object. The app estimates a string assembly path with a longest-repeat greedy algorithm, like the original AssemblyExplainer. Optimal assembly is hard; this is a fast pedagogical approximation.

    § causal funnel

    Assembly space is huge. History picks a path.

    The paper's big philosophical move is to treat novelty and contingency as physical. The possible space of objects explodes combinatorially. But observed lineages are narrow: chemistry, life, intelligence, and technology keep reusing discovered parts.

    Medium-hard version, not just a blog post: this page has an editable phase plot, threshold controls, a live selection simulation, a string assembly estimator, and a causal-space visualization. The missing hard-hard step would be real molecular assembly-index computation from mass-spec or graph data.
    § caveat

    This is a map of the argument, not proof of the territory.

    Assembly Theory is ambitious and contested. Critics argue that parts of it resemble compression, algorithmic information, or known complexity measures. The strongest practical claim is narrower and testable: high molecular assembly plus high copy number may be a useful biosignature. This app tries to make the intuition visible without pretending the debate is settled.
