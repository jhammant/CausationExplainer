The Causation Meter.

If you find one complicated thing, it might be luck. If you find many identical complicated things, something has been making them. Assembly Theory turns that intuition into a proposed physical scale: assembly index × copy number.

§ paper in 90 seconds

The argument, in one guided pass.

Cronin and Walker’s move is to treat causation as something matter can carry. An object’s assembly index counts the shortest recursive history needed to build it. Copy number asks whether that history happened once, or whether some persistent mechanism keeps making the same object again.

In finite time, blind chemistry can make simple things in huge numbers and occasionally make rare complicated things. What it should not make is many identical high-assembly objects. That top-right region — high complexity, high abundance — is where selection, biology, agency, or technology becomes the better explanation.

The app below follows that structure: first the threshold, then the space of possibilities, then the measurement problem, then the selection ratchet.

The paper argues that causation is not just a story we tell after the fact. It can be measured in matter. A thing with a high assembly index embodies a long chain of contingent construction steps. A large copy number says that chain is not a one-off accident: some persistent mechanism is producing it.

The interesting zone is not merely complexity. Random strings can be complex. Nor merely abundance. Simple salts can be everywhere. It is the combination: copied objects that are hard to make.

A ≈ Σ nᵢ eaᵢ / N

Toy version used here: assembly field rises when objects have both high assembly index aᵢ and high copy number nᵢ. The real theory is more careful; this app is a visual intuition pump, not a lab instrument.

    Fragment evidence

    What would make this genuinely hard-hard?

    1. Parse SMILES/InChI or molecular graphs and canonicalise repeated subgraphs.
    2. Search approximate minimal assembly paths over graph joins, with pruning and known chemistry constraints.
    3. Use mass-spec fragmentation trees to infer which substructures are actually observed.
    4. Combine assembly estimate, copy number, instrument confidence, and sample controls into a biosignature score.

    Open paper PDF

    Assembly Theory is ambitious and contested. Critics argue that parts of it resemble compression, algorithmic information, or known complexity measures. The strongest practical claim is narrower and testable: high molecular assembly plus high copy number may be a useful biosignature. This app tries to make the intuition visible without pretending the debate is settled.
